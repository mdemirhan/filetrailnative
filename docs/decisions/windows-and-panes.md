# Decisions: Windows, tabs, panes and menus

Two panes, workspaces, tabs, the menus and their keys, Back and Forward, the sidebar and Settings. Part of the owner's decisions: [the index](../decisions.md) lists the other parts, and [principles.md](../principles.md) says how a decision is weighed and when to ask about one.

- **A tab can show two panes side by side.** Each pane has its own folder, history, view, order, Hidden Files, Folders First, filter, search and selection; the toolbar, the tree, the Info panel and the commands follow the active pane, so what the other pane does (open a folder, go Back or Forward, switch its view or order, search, select) changes only the tree's marks, its own Trash bar and what the commands can do with it; the active pane has the blue selection, a short accent line along its top and its path in full color. View ▸ Two Panes (⌘\\) opens the second pane where it was last closed in the tab, or on the active pane's folder, and closes the pane that isn't active. Tab walks the tree, the left pane and the right pane in that order (⇧Tab back; a hidden tree is left out). Copy and Move to Other Pane are F5 and F6 only, so the menu shows no key for them: ⇧⌘C and ⇧⌘X were dropped, as ⇧⌘X a key away from ⌘X would move files at once. They sit in the Edit menu in a group of their own, after Cut, Copy and Paste, as AppKit lets an item with ⇧ take the press of the same key without it, should anyone give them ⇧⌘ keys. When a copy, a move or a drag ends, both panes of the tab are read at once, rather than the one not active waiting for the folder watch, and what arrived is selected in the pane that shows it. The right-click menus have one Other Pane submenu, only while the tab shows two panes: Open, Copy and Move for items, and Show This Folder and Go to Its Folder on the list's background; an item that can't run now is dimmed, not hidden, so the submenu keeps its shape. A folder in the tree or the favorites has one thing to do there, so its menu has Open in Other Pane itself, not a submenu of one item. Open in Other Pane is ⌥⌘↓ and Swap Panes ⌘U; a swap keeps the active pane active, on the other side, and the keyboard where it was: in the tree, a field (a path being typed goes on), or either pane's list, which a right-click in the other pane can leave it in. The tab keeps one tree for both panes: it follows the active pane, marks the folders both panes show, and a pane switch never moves its root; ⌥-click or ⌥Return opens a folder in the other pane, which stays inactive. Panes are side by side only. A saved tab keeps its second pane, the active pane and the divider in optional fields, so a file from before panes reads as one pane; the divider is kept with each tab. The Two Panes button is in the default toolbar; a toolbar saved before it doesn't get it. New Window and New Tab open with one pane; Duplicate Tab, Reopen Closed Tab, Move Tab to New Window and Merge All Windows keep both. The Info Row shows in the active pane only and moves with it. When a disk is ejected, a pane showing it goes Home, and the tab closes only when every pane of it was on that disk; a tab whose tree alone was rooted there stays, with the tree at Home. Copy and Move to Other Pane do nothing with both panes on one folder, from the menu, which dims them, and from F5 and F6 alike. Return acts in the list that has the keyboard, the other pane's too after a right-click there. Copy and Move to Other Pane are dimmed while the other pane shows the Trash, in the Edit menu and in the right-click menu alike; Go to Other Pane's Folder is not, as going there writes nothing, nor while it shows search results. Closing the other pane changes nothing of the active one: the sidebar's row and Show Info's item stay. A drag from one pane onto the other's list goes into the folder it shows, on its background as on its folders, search results included, and the pane that takes it becomes active.
- **⌘W closes the pane it is in first.** In a tab with two panes, File ▸ Close Pane (⌘W) closes the active pane: the other takes the whole width and becomes active, with the keyboard if the closed one had it, and ⌘\ opens the closed one again on the right, at its folder and history. With one pane the same item is Close Tab, and on the last tab it closes the window, as before. Close Tab (⌥⌘W), the item ⌥ shows in its place, closes the tab with both its panes; so do the tab's close button and the window's. A rename being typed or a dialog open holds ⌘W, as it holds Close Tab. ⌘W was Close Tab in every tab: one press put away both panes, the one not being looked at included, with only Reopen Closed Tab to bring them back. Closing the innermost thing first is what ⌘W does one level up (the last tab closes the window) and in apps with independent splits; the cost is a second press to close a tab with two panes. A key chosen for Close Tab before stays Close Tab's, so whoever gave it ⌘W keeps ⌘W closing the whole tab; and whoever gave Close Tab other keys then, or none, took ⌘W off closing a tab on purpose, so Close Pane, which closes a tab of one pane too, has no key for them until they give it one or reset it. Settings → Shortcuts says under each what it closes. With Settings or Help in front the File menu's item reads Close Tab whatever the tab behind shows, as ⌘W closes that window. Decided 2026-11-15.
- **The menus keep Finder's order and groups, and what Finder doesn't have sits beside the item of Finder's it is most like, in the menu it would be looked for.** File goes Open and the other ways to open; Calculate Size and Rename (Finder's Get Info and Rename); Compress, Extract and Extract To; Duplicate, Move to…, Convert Images… and Quick Look; Add to Favorites, Open in Terminal and Show in Finder; Move to Trash and Eject. Edit keeps Cut, Copy, Paste and Select All together, then what goes to and from the system's clipboard (Copy Path, Copy Contents, Paste as File), then Copy and Move to Other Pane. Window keeps Finder's four tab items together, with Switch to Other Pane after them. The commands for the two panes stay in the five menus they are in (File, Edit, View, Go, Window), each with the commands of its kind, not in a menu or submenu of their own. An item's right-click menu starts with Open and Open With, as Finder's does, and has Compress, Extract, Extract To and Convert Images in a group after Rename, Duplicate, Move to… and New Folder; the tag colors come after both.
- **The command palette lists every command.** ⇧⌘P, or View ▸ Command Palette…, drops a box below the toolbar, opaque like Go To since it holds a field and a list, with every row of the shortcut table and the menu items that aren't in it (About, Manage Workspaces…, each workspace by its name), so a command with keys but no menu item can be found too. ⇧⌘P is the palette's key in Sublime Text and VS Code; ⌘P is Go to File there and Print on the Mac, and Finder's ⇧⌘P is its preview column, which the app has no command for. A row has the command's name (the table's label, or the menu's title where it names what the command acts on), where it is in the menus, its keys as Settings has them and a check for what is on. Under the list, the selected command's line from Help's shortcut list says what it does; each says more than the command's name, so a command whose name isn't enough (Two Panes, Pin Filter, Copy Contents) is explained where it is found, and the lines that only repeated a name were rewritten, for Help too. A command that can't run now is dimmed, as the menu dims it, and listed after the ones that can; Return on it beeps. Hiding it would make the command seem missing. Typing matches the name as the list filter does in Fuzzy (letters in order, the accent rules), ordered by how well; the menu place matches too, after any name, so "go doc" finds Go ▸ Documents. Among commands that fit alike, the one named just what was typed comes first, so "copy" and Return run Copy and not Copy to Other Pane; then the one run from the palette most recently; and a command whose whole name begins another's comes before it ("dup" is Duplicate, then Duplicate Tab). In a tab with one pane Close Pane reads Close Tab, as the menu has it, and Close Tab, which would do the same there, is not listed a second time. With nothing typed the box lists up to five of those, then every command by group, as Settings does; the last 20 are kept in `recent-commands.json`. ↑↓ and ⌃P/⌃N move, Page Up and Page Down by a page, Return or a click runs, and Esc, ⌘., a click outside or the palette's key again closes; any key given to the palette in Settings opens and closes it, one the menu can't carry too (a second key, one with no ⌘ or ⌃). The pointer selects the row it is over only once it has clearly moved since the keyboard last chose a row (typing, an arrow, the box opening), so a hand brushing the trackpad on its way to Return doesn't change the command Return runs. A command that has no menu item and can't run now (Open Selected Item with nothing selected) beeps. The box closes before the command runs and gives the keyboard back to what had it, and the command goes as its menu item does, so it acts where it would from the menu. From Settings or Help, ⇧⌘P brings the browsing window in front and opens the box there; with none open it beeps. Nothing is typed into a command (Go To takes paths), and files and folders are not listed (Go To finds them). What a new command needs to be in it is in `docs/process/commands.md`.
- **A window's tabs can be saved by name as a workspace, and opened again.** A workspace is one window: its tabs with their panes (each pane's folder, view, order and sizes, the active pane, the divider), the tab in front, the folder tree and the Info panel with their widths, the column widths, and the window's size and whether it was zoomed, not its place on screen. The whole app is not one, as the app already brings back every window at launch. Window ▸ Workspaces has Save Window as Workspace…, the workspaces in their order and Manage Workspaces…; the list is in Settings ▸ Browsing, where workspaces are renamed, removed and put in order. A workspace opens in a new window, placed as New Window places one; with ⌥ the menu item opens it in this window instead, which takes the window's place and size and closes it. A window that a sheet, a dialog, an alert or a name being edited holds isn't replaced: the item beeps and nothing opens. One that comes to hold one while the workspace's first folder loads stays open too, under the workspace's window, with a beep: closing it would lose what is open in it, and the workspace was asked for. What is saved is a copy, and a window is not linked to the workspace it came from: the menu checks none, and there is no Update. A workspace changes only when a window is saved under its name: the name field of Save Window as Workspace… has a button, or ↓, that lists the saved names to choose one, and the line under the title gives the key of the workspace named. The link made the checked item look like the window it came from, though choosing it opens another, and saving over a workspace is rare enough to take a few steps. A workspace always opens as it was saved, in a new window, also while a window opened from it or saved as it is open: that window may have changed since, and bringing it to the front made the key seem not to work. The first five in the list open with ⌃⌘1 to ⌃⌘5, and in this window with ⌃⌥⌘1 to ⌃⌥⌘5; the keys go by the list's order, not by use, so a key never moves to another workspace. They are Workspace 1 to 5 and Workspace 1 to 5 in This Window in Settings ▸ Shortcuts, each changed on its own. macOS swaps a menu item for the one ⌥ shows only when their keys differ by ⌥ alone, so while they do, the item for this window is the one ⌥ shows; a key chosen that doesn't pair with its workspace's shows both items, each with its key. Save has no key, as ⌃⌘S is the folder tree's. A tab whose folder is gone opens the nearest folder above it, as a tab does when its folder goes, and a notice says how many did. Names are unique ignoring case and how an accented letter is stored (a folder's name on disk, which Save offers, may keep the accent apart from its letter, and typing composes them), are kept composed, and are up to 80 characters; saving under a name that is taken asks before replacing it. At most 50 workspaces are kept. Workspaces are kept with the preferences in `app-state.json`; a file from before them has none, and a window's `workspaceID` from when windows were linked is not read.
- **A tab dropped into a window that already has 25 goes back out** into a window of its own, with the tab-limit notice. The session save still cuts at 25, as the limit doesn't hold everywhere: a folder opened with the app at launch is added as a tab to a restored window even when it has 25, and that stays as it is.
- **Back and Forward keep the history with the folder on screen.** Back, Forward and the hold menus move the history's index before the folder is read, so two quick Backs go two folders back (they go to B and then A on [A, B, C]). While such a move hasn't arrived, the pane remembers where the folder on screen is in the history, and puts the index back there when a folder is opened, a read in place starts, or the move fails with the old folder still on screen; a move that is overtaken does nothing, and another Back, Forward or jump goes on from where the index is. So a folder opened while Back is still reading comes after the folder Back was pressed in, and Back to a folder that is gone leaves Back and Forward going on from the folder shown. Left as it was, Back to a slow folder and then another folder opened dropped the folder come from, and Back to a deleted folder left the old folder on screen with the index moved.
- **Back doesn't follow a folder renamed since it was visited, and Quick Look does nothing of its own when the window's folder changes.** Neither was wanted, and the unwired code for them went.
- **A click on a sidebar row opens its folder at once.** Nothing waits to see whether a second click follows; the second click of a double-click only opens or closes the folder's branch in the tree. With single-click expand on, the first click already does both, and the second does nothing more. A click on a path bar segment still waits 250 ms, as a double-click there edits the path.
- **File ▸ Remove from Favorites is offered for a Trash favorite**, as the context menu is.
- **An item opened from another app waits for the items given before it for 3 seconds at most.** One whose disk doesn't answer then lets the next go, and opens itself once its disk answers.
- **Double-click always opens.** "Double-click a file to: Edit" is gone: it sent every plain file to the text editor, zips, pictures and PDFs too. Edit is File ▸ Edit in… (⇧⌘E) and the file's context menu. A saved setting of it is ignored.
- **⌘⇧= is ⌘+** when a key is recorded or compared in Settings → Shortcuts, as most keyboards type + with Shift; not with ⌥, which types another character.
- **`constructor` and `__proto__` are unknown modifier names** in a shortcut, like any other unknown name.
- **Settings keeps its own row and card layout.** Its controls are the system's, but the page layout is not a control, and a dozen layout tests pin its frames, so `SettingsMetrics` keeps its layout constants.
- **Settings lists act on no row while none is selected**: ⌫, Return and Space do nothing, and an arrow selects the first row.
