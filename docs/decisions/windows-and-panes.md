# Decisions: Windows, tabs, panes and menus

Two panes, workspaces, tabs, the menus and their keys, Back and Forward, the sidebar and Settings. Part of the owner's decisions: [the index](../decisions.md) lists the other parts, and [principles.md](../principles.md) says how a decision is weighed and when to ask about one.

- **A tab can show two panes side by side.** Each pane has its own folder, history, view, order, Folders First, filter, search and selection; the toolbar, the tree, the Info panel, the Info Row and the commands follow the active pane. Panes are side by side only. What was decided:
  - **The tab keeps one tree for both panes**: it follows the active pane, marks the folders both panes show, and a pane switch never moves its root. ⌥-click or ⌥Return in it opens a folder in the other pane, which stays inactive.
  - **Hidden Files is the tab's, not a pane's.** The tree is the tab's and would otherwise change at each pane switch, and hidden items are wanted for what is being done in the tab (a `.git`, a `.env`), not in one folder of it, as Finder's ⇧⌘. is one setting too. A folder's view leaves hidden files out for the same reason (see "A folder can keep its own view"). A tab saved while each pane had its own takes its active pane's.
  - **Turning Hidden Files on or off keeps each pane's selection and scroll**, but for a hidden item that goes: the items are the same but for the hidden ones, as after a Refresh. Until 2026-11-15 it emptied the selection and went back to the top, as another order does.
  - **Two Panes (⌘\\) opens the second pane where it was last closed in the tab, or else as a copy of the active pane**, and closes the pane that isn't active. Coming back where it was is wanted for going back and forth between two places. A copy has the active pane's folder, view, order, Folders First, sizes, filter mode and Back and Forward, as Duplicate Tab copies a tab; what is typed and what is selected stay behind. It copies the filter mode, though a new tab starts in the mode chosen last, as it is a copy of the pane beside it. Go ▸ Show This Folder in Other Pane opens the same copy while the tab has one pane.
  - **Duplicate Pane (⌥⌘\\) always opens the copy** (2026-11-18), and with two panes puts it in place of the other one, on its side and at its width. Starting a pane from where one is had no key. It is named after Duplicate Tab; Two Panes Here was the first name, but ⌘\\ opens a pane on the same folder too, so "here" didn't tell the two apart. It was first the item ⌥ shows in Two Panes' place, and is an item of its own since, as an item that only ⌥ shows is hard to find. Its toolbar button is on no toolbar until it is added.
  - **Copy and Move to Other Pane are F5 and F6 only**, which the window handles; the menus show them beside the items and never take them (the owner, 2026-11-21: before, the menus showed no key for them). ⇧⌘C and ⇧⌘X were dropped, as ⇧⌘X a key away from ⌘X would move files at once. They sit in the Edit menu in a group of their own, after Cut, Copy and Paste, as AppKit lets an item with ⇧ take the press of the same key without it, should anyone give them ⇧⌘ keys. They do nothing with both panes on one folder, from the menu, which dims them, and from the keys alike, and are dimmed while the other pane shows the Trash; Go to Other Pane's Folder is not, as going there writes nothing, nor while it shows search results.
  - **Open in Other Pane is ⌥⌘↓ and Swap Panes ⌘U.** A swap keeps the active pane active, on the other side, and the keyboard where it was. Tab walks the tree, the left pane and the right pane in that order (⇧Tab back; a hidden tree is left out).
  - **The right-click menus have one Other Pane submenu, only while the tab shows two panes.** An item that can't run now is dimmed, not hidden, so the submenu keeps its shape. A folder in the tree or the favorites has one thing to do there, so its menu has Open in Other Pane itself, not a submenu of one item. **With one pane, the right-click menus have Open in Other Pane**, which opens the second: for a folder of the tree or the favorites, and for one folder or one file of a list, the file shown there in the viewer (the owner, 2026-11-20: with one pane the menu had no way to it).
  - **When a copy, a move or a drag ends, both panes are read at once**, rather than the one not active waiting for the folder watch, and what arrived is selected in the pane that shows it. A drag from one pane onto the other's list goes into the folder it shows, search results included, and the pane that takes it becomes active.
  - **New Window and New Tab open with one pane**; Duplicate Tab, Reopen Closed Tab, Move Tab to New Window and Merge All Windows keep both. A saved tab keeps its second pane, the active pane and the divider in optional fields, so a file from before panes reads as one pane. The Two Panes button is in the default toolbar; a toolbar saved before it doesn't get it.
  - **When a disk is ejected, a pane showing it goes Home**, and the tab closes only when every pane of it was on that disk; a tab whose tree alone was rooted there stays, with the tree at Home.
  - Closing the other pane changes nothing of the active one. Return acts in the list that has the keyboard, the other pane's too after a right-click there.
- **The default toolbar is groups in pills of their own, and only the title and a search field are always there** (2026-11-17). A toolbar not customized holds the folder tree; Back, Forward, the title and the clipboard; the View Mode Menu and Sort; Search; View Options, Two Panes and the Info panel, each group a glass pill of its own. A toolbar saved before keeps its items. View Options is an item like the others since then: it had been held on the toolbar since the first version, as its menu was once the only way back to customizing the toolbar; View ▸ Customize Toolbar… and a right-click on the toolbar are that now, and everything else in its menu is in the View menu. The title and a search field stay: the title says where the window is, and ⌘F needs a search item on the toolbar.
- **A line along the top of the active pane says where the keyboard is** (2026-11-15), with one pane as with two: the accent with a narrow glow while the pane has the keyboard, the accent at 45% with no glow while the keyboard is elsewhere in the window, grey while the window is in the background. It follows the same signal as the selection, so the two always agree, and it shows where the keyboard is with nothing selected, where the selection can't. It stays on the active pane while the sidebar has the keyboard, as the tree is driving that pane. It used to show with two panes only, always in the accent, bright in a background window too.
  - **What is drawn under it starts 10 pt below the pane's top** (`DesignTokens.paneContentTop`), clear of the line and the bright part of its glow. The search bar's blue scope had sat on the line; one rule keeps what is added under the line later from crowding it too.
  - **Considered and dropped**: a line over every pane (both dimmed while the sidebar has the keyboard, which hides the active pane); a fainter line over the pane that isn't active (tried in the app, the version without it read better); a line that moves into the sidebar (the sidebar has no edge at the panes' height, so a line there floats); a border or focus ring round the pane (the drop target's ring is one already); a tint over the sidebar; a rounded glass rim, a capsule, and a wide glow down from the toolbar.
- **⌘W closes the pane it is in first** (2026-11-15). In a tab with two panes, File ▸ Close Pane (⌘W) closes the active pane and the other takes the whole width; with one pane the same item is Close Tab, and on the last tab it closes the window, as before. Close Tab (⌥⌘W), the tab's close button and the window's close the tab with both its panes. ⌘W was Close Tab in every tab: one press put away both panes, the one not being looked at included. Closing the innermost thing first is what ⌘W does one level up (the last tab closes the window) and in apps with independent splits; the cost is a second press to close a tab with two panes.
  - **A key chosen for Close Tab before stays Close Tab's**, so whoever gave it ⌘W keeps ⌘W closing the whole tab. Whoever gave Close Tab other keys, or none, took ⌘W off closing a tab on purpose, so Close Pane has no key for them until they give it one or reset it. Settings → Shortcuts says under each what it closes.
  - A rename being typed or a dialog open holds ⌘W, as it holds Close Tab. With Settings or Help in front the item reads Close Tab, as ⌘W closes that window.
- **A closed tab or pane comes back as it was** (2026-11-18). Reopen Closed Tab (⇧⌘T) brings back each pane's folder, view, order, sizes, filters, selection and scroll, its Back and Forward with the place in each folder of the history, the tree, the two panes and the divider, where the tab was in the row. A pane closed with ⌘W or ⌘\\ comes back with ⌘\\ the same way. A tab used to come back with its folder only and a Back of nothing, after the tab on screen: undoing a ⌘W lost the place in the folder and the way back to the folders before it.
  - **A search showing as it closed doesn't come back**, as it would run again, nor a read error that was on screen, as the folder is read again.
  - **A pane closed inside a tab is not part of the tab**: the tab reopened has none to bring back, so ⌘\\ in it opens a copy of the active pane, as in a new tab.
  - **Neither closed tabs nor closed panes are kept between launches.** (No reason recorded.)
  - Duplicate Tab carries the second pane's selection, filters and scroll too, as it did the first pane's.
- **The menus keep Finder's order and groups, and what Finder doesn't have sits beside the item of Finder's it is most like, in the menu it would be looked for.** So Calculate Size sits where Finder has Get Info; Compress, Extract and Extract To stay together; Deselect All (⌥⌘A, Finder's key) and Invert Selection follow Select All, Deselect All as an item of its own where Finder shows it with ⌥; and what goes to and from the system's clipboard (Copy Path, Copy Contents, Paste as File) follows Cut, Copy and Paste. The commands for the two panes stay in the five menus they are in (File, Edit, View, Go, Window), each with the commands of its kind, not in a menu or submenu of their own. Window keeps Finder's four tab items together, with Switch to Other Pane after them. An item's right-click menu starts with Open and Open With, as Finder's does, and has Compress, Extract, Extract To and Convert Images in a group after Rename, Duplicate, Move to… and New Folder; the tag colors come after both. Share…, Save as Template, Add to Favorites, Open in Terminal and Show in Finder are one group after them, what hands the items to something else and leaves them as they are (the owner, 2026-11-21).
- **The File menu is in the right-click menu's groups** (the owner, 2026-11-21, after a review of every menu): opening and looking (Open, Open With ▸, Open in Viewer, the Open in … items, Edit in, Quick Look, Calculate Size, Reveal in Folder, Show Original); what changes the items (Rename, Duplicate, Make Alias, Move to…); what makes files of them (Compress, Extract, Extract To…, Convert Images…); what hands them on (Share…, Add to Favorites, Open in Terminal, Show in Finder); then Move to Trash, with Delete Immediately… as the item ⌥ shows in its place, and Eject. Calculate Size is with opening and looking as it changes nothing. Open With ▸ is in File as in Finder's. Edit keeps its clipboard items at the top level: no submenu for them.
- **A menu shows a key the window handles, and never takes it.** The menu bar takes only keys with ⌘ or ⌃ (`ApplicationMainMenu`), so an item may show F2, F5, F6 or ↩ while a text field or a dialog keeps them. Rename shows ↩ while Return renames, as Help and the right-click menu do, and F2 while it opens. The right-click View As and Sort By submenus show the keys View has.
- **Default keys added** (the owner, 2026-11-21): Edit in Text Editor ⇧⌘E (back from before ⌘E went to Open in Viewer), Add to Favorites ⌃⌘T (Finder's Add to Sidebar), and Delete Immediately… ⌥⌘⌫, Finder's, for what is selected in the Trash only, always asking Finder's question first; outside the Trash the key does nothing, whichever way it arrives. Finder's Sort By and Group By keys were left out: they are the workspaces' keys here.
- **A long name in a menu loses its middle, so the menu keeps its width.** A menu is as wide as its widest item, and a file named with 90 letters made the right-click menu as wide as the screen. Past 24 letters (a Chinese, Japanese or Korean letter or an emoji counts as two) a name keeps its start, its last five letters and its extension around "…", as Finder's menus do, in every menu that names a file, a tab, a workspace or a preset. Only the menus are shortened: the undo history, notices, tooltips and titles keep the whole name.
- **The command palette lists every command.** ⇧⌘P, or View ▸ Command Palette…, lists every row of the shortcut table and the menu items that aren't in it, so a command with keys but no menu item can be found too. Each row has the command's name, where it is in the menus, its keys and a check for what is on, and the selected command's line from Help says what it does. What a new command needs to be in it is in `docs/process/commands.md`. What was decided:
  - **⇧⌘P** is the palette's key in Sublime Text and VS Code; ⌘P is Go to File there and Print on the Mac, and Finder's ⇧⌘P is its preview column, which the app has no command for.
  - **A command that can't run now is dimmed**, as the menu dims it, and listed after the ones that can; Return on it beeps. Hiding it would make the command seem missing.
  - **Typing matches the name as the list filter does in Fuzzy**, and the menu place after any name, so "go doc" finds Go ▸ Documents. Among commands that fit alike, the one named just what was typed comes first, so "copy" and Return run Copy and not Copy to Other Pane; then the one run from the palette most recently; then a command whose whole name begins another's. With nothing typed it lists up to five run lately, then every command by group, as Settings does.
  - **Help's lines say more than the command's name**, so a command whose name isn't enough (Two Panes, Pin Filter, Copy Contents) is explained where it is found; the lines that only repeated a name were rewritten, for Help too.
  - **The pointer selects the row it is over only once it has clearly moved** since the keyboard last chose a row, so a hand brushing the trackpad on its way to Return doesn't change the command Return runs.
  - **The command goes as its menu item does**: the box closes first and gives the keyboard back to what had it, so the command acts where it would from the menu. From Settings or Help, ⇧⌘P brings the browsing window in front and opens the box there.
  - **Nothing is typed into a command** (Go To takes paths), **and files and folders are not listed** (Go To finds them).
  - In a tab with one pane Close Pane reads Close Tab, and Close Tab, which would do the same there, is not listed a second time. Any key given to the palette in Settings opens and closes it.
- **A window's tabs can be saved by name as a workspace, and opened again.** Window ▸ Workspaces saves, lists and opens them; Settings ▸ Browsing renames, removes and orders them. What was decided:
  - **A workspace is one window**: its tabs with their panes, views and sizes, the tab in front, the folder tree and the Info panel with their widths, and the window's size, not its place on screen. The whole app is not one, as the app already brings back every window at launch.
  - **What is saved is a copy, and a window is not linked to the workspace it came from**: the menu checks none, and there is no Update. The link made the checked item look like the window it came from, though choosing it opens another, and saving over a workspace is rare enough to take a few steps: a workspace changes only when a window is saved under its name, which asks before replacing it.
  - **A workspace always opens as it was saved, in a new window**, also while a window opened from it is open: that window may have changed since, and bringing it to the front made the key seem not to work. With ⌥ it opens in this window instead, which it replaces.
  - **A window that a sheet, a dialog, an alert or a name being edited holds isn't replaced**: closing it would lose what is open in it. The item beeps, or, when the window came to hold one while the workspace loaded, the workspace opens in a window of its own, as it was asked for.
  - **The first five open with ⌃⌘1 to ⌃⌘5, and in this window with ⌃⌥⌘1 to ⌃⌥⌘5.** The keys go by the list's order, not by use, so a key never moves to another workspace. Each can be changed on its own; a key that doesn't pair with its workspace's by ⌥ alone shows both menu items, as macOS swaps an item for the one ⌥ shows only then. Save has no key, as ⌃⌘S is the folder tree's.
  - **Names are unique ignoring case and how an accented letter is stored**, as a folder's name on disk, which Save offers, may keep the accent apart from its letter and typing composes them. Names are up to 80 characters, and at most 50 workspaces are kept.
  - **A tab whose folder is gone opens the nearest folder above it**, as a tab does when its folder goes, and a notice says how many did.
  - Workspaces are kept with the preferences in `app-state.json`; a window's `workspaceID` from when windows were linked is not read.
- **A tab dropped into a window that already has 25 goes back out** into a window of its own, with the tab-limit notice. The session save still cuts at 25, as the limit doesn't hold everywhere: a folder opened with the app at launch is added as a tab to a restored window even when it has 25, and that stays as it is.
- **Back and Forward keep the history with the folder on screen.** Back, Forward and the hold menus move the history's index before the folder is read, so two quick Backs go two folders back. While such a move hasn't arrived, the pane remembers where the folder on screen is in the history, and puts the index back there when a folder is opened, a read in place starts, or the move fails with the old folder still on screen. Left as it was, Back to a slow folder and then another folder opened dropped the folder come from, and Back to a deleted folder left the old folder on screen with the index moved.
- **Back doesn't follow a folder renamed since it was visited, and Quick Look does nothing of its own when the window's folder changes.** Neither was wanted, and the unwired code for them went.
- **A click on a sidebar row opens its folder at once.** Nothing waits to see whether a second click follows; the second click of a double-click only opens or closes the folder's branch in the tree. With single-click expand on, the first click already does both, and the second does nothing more. A click on a path bar segment still waits 250 ms, as a double-click there edits the path.
- **File ▸ Remove from Favorites is offered for a Trash favorite**, as the context menu is.
- **An item opened from another app waits for the items given before it for 3 seconds at most.** One whose disk doesn't answer then lets the next go, and opens itself once its disk answers.
- **Double-click always opens.** "Double-click a file to: Edit" is gone: it sent every plain file to the text editor, zips, pictures and PDFs too. Edit is File ▸ Edit in… (⇧⌘E) and the file's context menu. A saved setting of it is ignored.
- **⌘⇧= is ⌘+** when a key is recorded or compared in Settings → Shortcuts, as most keyboards type + with Shift; not with ⌥, which types another character.
- **`constructor` and `__proto__` are unknown modifier names** in a shortcut, like any other unknown name.
- **Settings keeps its own row and card layout.** Its controls are the system's, but the page layout is not a control, and a dozen layout tests pin its frames, so `SettingsMetrics` keeps its layout constants.
- **Settings lists act on no row while none is selected**: ⌫, Return and Space do nothing, and an arrow selects the first row.
- **The app's own files are written at most twice a minute** (2026-11-19). `app-state.json`, `folder-views.json` and the files beside them share one limit: a deliberate change (a size, a column, a setting, a folder's view saved) is written about 1 s later, and after that no sooner than 30 s after the last write. Where the user goes (the folder, the view, the tabs, the window) waits up to 5 minutes, as before. What is unwritten is written at quit, and a file whose text hasn't changed is not written again; a crash loses at most 30 s of changes. The owner asked for writes that changes of view cause to be few: before, a change was written 150 ms after each pause, so ⌘+ pressed six times could write the state file six times.
- **The toolbar's title is in the label's color** (2026-11-20). It is the window's title, and dimmed only while the window isn't key. It was Finder's grey, measured from Finder (0.70 black where the label is 0.847), which read as disabled beside the toolbar's black symbols.
- **The About window is as high as what it shows** (2026-11-20): 460 wide, the footer 22 pt under the buttons. It was 356 high whatever it showed, with a band of nothing between them.
- **A list in Settings with nothing in it says so**: Workspaces reads "No workspaces yet. Save one from Window ▸ Workspaces." where its rows would be, as its buttons over nothing said little (2026-11-20).
