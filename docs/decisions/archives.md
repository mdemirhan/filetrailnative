# Decisions: Archives

Compress, Extract and Extract To, and the plan to browse archives. Part of the owner's decisions: [the index](../decisions.md) lists the other parts, and [principles.md](../principles.md) says how a decision is weighed and when to ask about one.

- **Archives are made and taken apart with the system's libarchive.** macOS ships it (`/usr/lib/libarchive.2.dylib`, behind `/usr/bin/tar`) without its headers, so its two headers are kept in `Sources/CFileTrail/libarchive` and nothing is added to the app; only formats it reads by itself are turned on, so no other program is run. File ▸ Compress makes a zip as Finder's Compress does, each item's extended attributes and resource fork kept in `__MACOSX` for `ditto` and Archive Utility to put back. Extract reads zip, tar (plain or with gzip, bzip2 or xz), 7z, RAR and xar, and a single file compressed with gzip, bzip2 or xz; an archive holding one item gives it, one holding several a folder named after the archive, and a name taken the next number. What is extracted is made out of sight and put in place whole, gets the archive's quarantine, loses setuid bits, and leaves out names reaching outside the folder. Names come out composed ("é" as one character), as Archive Utility gives them and as other systems read them, whichever way the archive spells them; nothing comes out writable by anyone but its owner, so a zip made on Windows gives 644 and not 664; and a hard link in an archive is a second file on a disk that keeps no hard links (exFAT, FAT), where the archive would otherwise fail whole. Compress leaves out the `._` files such a disk keeps attributes in, as they are the attributes of the items already in the zip, and names the item it failed on; an extraction that ends has all of its progress. Access rules (ACLs) an archive keeps are not put on what comes out, as one could keep the quarantine off or the items from being deleted; an archive whose items can't be given its quarantine fails, and leaves nothing, but on a disk that keeps no extended attributes at all its items come out without it, as Finder copies them there. Protected archives are refused for now, with Archive Utility named. Opening an archive (double-click, ⌘↓, Return when it opens, File ▸ Open, the context menu's Open) extracts it here, several in one run, rather than handing it to Archive Utility; only Open With hands it to an app. A key held down (Return, ⌘↓, ⌘O) extracts it once, as it mounts a disk image once: its repeats open folders and files as before, but extract and mount nothing, and once the press has extracted or mounted they open nothing at all until the key is let go, as what came out is selected by then and would be opened. A folder or a package named as an archive is, opened with other items, is handed to the system like them. An archive whose data isn't what it says it holds (a checksum that doesn't match) fails and leaves nothing, but for one compressed with gzip, whose checksum libarchive doesn't look at; a compressed file that holds an empty file comes out empty. A hole an archive names (a sparse file: a disk image) costs nothing on a disk that keeps holes, whatever its length; on one that doesn't it is refused when the disk hasn't room for it, and otherwise written a piece at a time, so Stop ends it. A folder that takes nothing new refuses before anything is extracted or compressed. What it gives is selected and scrolled to when it went into a folder a pane of the tab shows, and otherwise opens in a new window or tab, as "Open disk images and archives in" says: one for each folder something went into, with all of it selected there (ten archives extracted to one folder open one tab), and a folder that came out alone in its folder on itself. A link that came out is a file here: what it points to isn't looked at. `.tar.zst` and `.tar.lz4` are not offered: the system's libarchive reads them only with a `zstd` or `lz4` tool installed. File Trail doesn't list itself in Finder's Open With for archives. libarchive only reads an archive and writes a zip: the app itself puts what comes out on the disk and walks the folders it compresses, as libarchive's own way of doing either changes the whole app's umask or working folder for a moment, under every other thread. An item of an archive that is no file, folder or link (a device, a pipe), a second item of a name already written, and one whose path runs through a link are left out and counted, not a reason to stop; a name that isn't UTF-8 keeps its bytes as `%XX`, as the archive doesn't say what they were written in. A link to an archive, opened, gives its items beside the link.
- **Archives will be browsed as read-only folders** (not built yet). Opening an archive will browse it; whether a setting lets it extract instead, as today, is decided then. "Opening in Their App" is no setting: Open With does it. The context menu always offers the other ways. One archive opens in place, as a folder does; several each open in a new tab, under the open-item limit, and when the window hasn't room for all of their tabs none opens and one notice says so. Documents that are zips (docx, epub, jar) open in their apps whatever the setting, with Browse Contents in their context menu. Inside an archive nothing is changed. The plan, and where Phase 1 differs from Finder, is in [archives.md](../plans/archives.md).
