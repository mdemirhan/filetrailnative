# Decisions: Archives

Compress, Extract and Extract To, and the plan to browse archives. Part of the owner's decisions: [the index](../decisions.md) lists the other parts, and [principles.md](../principles.md) says how a decision is weighed and when to ask about one.

- **Archives are made and taken apart with the system's libarchive.** macOS ships it without its headers, so its two headers are kept in `Sources/CFileTrail/libarchive` and nothing is added to the app; only the formats it reads by itself are turned on, so no other program is run. That is why `.tar.zst` and `.tar.lz4` are not offered: the system's libarchive reads them only with a `zstd` or `lz4` tool installed. What was decided around it:
  - **libarchive only reads an archive and writes a zip.** The app itself puts what comes out on the disk and walks the folders it compresses, as libarchive's own way of doing either changes the whole app's umask or working folder for a moment, under every other thread.
  - **Compress makes a zip as Finder's does**, each item's extended attributes and resource fork kept in `__MACOSX` for `ditto` and Archive Utility to put back. It leaves out the `._` files a disk without attributes keeps them in, as they are the attributes of items already in the zip. Only on such a disk: elsewhere a `._name` file is someone's own and goes in (2026-11-20; it was left out on every disk without a word).
  - **Extract reads** zip, tar (plain or with gzip, bzip2 or xz), 7z, RAR and xar, and a single file compressed with gzip, bzip2 or xz. An archive holding one item gives it, one holding several a folder named after the archive, and a name taken the next number. It is made out of sight and put in place whole.
  - **What comes out can't do more than the archive's own file could.** It gets the archive's quarantine and loses setuid bits; nothing is writable by anyone but its owner (a zip made on Windows gives 644, not 664); names reaching outside the folder are left out; a link that came out is a file, and what it points to isn't looked at. Access rules (ACLs) in an archive are not applied, as one could keep the quarantine off or the items from being deleted. An archive whose items can't be given its quarantine fails and leaves nothing, with two exceptions. On a disk that keeps no extended attributes at all they come out without it, as Finder copies them there. On a disk that keeps attributes in `._name` files (exFAT, FAT) a `._name` file of the archive's own, which the system takes for another item's attributes and gives none of its own, is left out and counted, as Compress leaves such files out there (2026-11-20; a downloaded zip holding such a file before its item was refused whole).
  - **A bad item is left out and counted, not a reason to stop**: one that is no file, folder or link (a device, a pipe), a second item of a name already written, and one whose path runs through a link. A hard link is a second file on a disk that keeps no hard links (exFAT, FAT), where the archive would otherwise fail whole.
  - **Names come out composed** ("é" as one character), as Archive Utility gives them and as other systems read them, whichever way the archive spells them. A zip's names that aren't UTF-8 are read in the encoding they fit (Shift JIS, GB18030, Big5, CP949 or Windows-1252, the user's languages first, else CP437, the format's own), as zips from Japanese, Chinese and Korean Windows are common and Archive Utility guesses too (2026-11-20; they came out as `%XX`). That encoding is the zip's, not each name's: every name the zip doesn't mark as UTF-8 is read in it, also one whose bytes happen to be UTF-8 as they stand, and one it can't read is left out and counted (2026-11-20; such a name was kept as UTF-8, so `目录` in GBK came out as `Ŀ¼`, a second folder beside the one the zip's other items went into). In other archives, or where no encoding fits, such bytes stay `%XX`.
  - **Data that isn't what the archive says it holds (a checksum that doesn't match) fails and leaves nothing**, but for gzip, whose checksum libarchive doesn't look at. A folder that takes nothing new refuses before anything is extracted or compressed.
  - **A hole an archive names (a sparse file: a disk image) costs nothing** on a disk that keeps holes. On one that doesn't it is refused when the disk hasn't room for it, and otherwise written a piece at a time, so Stop ends it.
  - **Protected archives are refused for now**, with Archive Utility named.
  - **Opening an archive extracts it here**, several in one run, rather than handing it to Archive Utility; only Open With hands it to an app, and File Trail doesn't list itself in Finder's Open With for archives. A folder or a package named as an archive is, opened with other items, is handed to the system like them. A link to an archive gives its items beside the link.
  - **A key held down extracts once**, as it mounts a disk image once. After that its repeats open nothing until the key is let go, as what came out is selected by then and would be opened.
  - **What came out is selected and scrolled to** when it went into a folder a pane of the tab shows, and otherwise opens in a new window or tab, as "Open disk images and archives in" says: one for each folder something went into (ten archives extracted to one folder open one tab), and a folder that came out alone opens on itself.
- **Archives will be browsed as read-only folders** (not built yet). Opening an archive will browse it; whether a setting lets it extract instead, as today, is decided then. "Opening in Their App" is no setting: Open With does it. The context menu always offers the other ways. One archive opens in place, as a folder does; several each open in a new tab, under the open-item limit, and when the window hasn't room for all of their tabs none opens and one notice says so. Documents that are zips (docx, epub, jar) open in their apps whatever the setting, with Browse Contents in their context menu. Inside an archive nothing is changed. The plan, and where Phase 1 differs from Finder, is in [archives.md](../plans/archives.md).
