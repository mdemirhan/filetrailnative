# Decisions: Views and what they show

The List, Icons, Compact List and Previews views, their sizes, Quick Look, and how items are named and drawn. Part of the owner's decisions: [the index](../decisions.md) lists the other parts, and [principles.md](../principles.md) says how a decision is weighed and when to ask about one.

- **List view's columns keep the widths they were given.** A pane narrower than the columns (a narrow window, the Info panel open) scrolls sideways; no column shrinks or hides to fit, and Name is never squeezed. A column dragged past the pane's edge scrolls the list so its divider stays in view. Widths change only when someone drags, steps or double-clicks a divider, so what is set is what shows.
- **The Icons view's icon size and the Compact List's column width are set with one slider in the status bar**, and with View ▸ Make Bigger, Make Smaller and Default Size (⌘+, ⌘- and ⌘0, changeable in Settings; ⌘= is ⌘+'s other key, as most keyboards type + with Shift): icons from 32 to 128 pt in steps of 8, columns from 160 to 600 pt, the commands going to the next whole 40. The List has none. Each pane has its own sizes, at each density, with the Previews view's count (see "Each pane has its own sizes"). The items are named for the view ("Make Icons Bigger", "Make Columns Wider") and are dimmed in the List and at the ends of the range. While the slider moves, or after one of the commands, the size shows in points in place of the status text, and fades once it stops. Dragging the gaps between columns was tried and dropped: nothing on screen shows the gaps can be dragged. Icons larger than 64 pt ask Quick Look for 256-pixel previews instead of 128. An item's column is its icon plus 40 pt, 104 at the default 64, and grows and narrows with the icon, but never below 80 pt: smaller icons fit more items across, and a name still has a 78 pt label for its two lines. Holding the column at 104 below 64 pt, so smaller icons only made the rows shorter, was what there was; no floor at all would leave a name 70 pt at 32 pt icons.
- **Each pane has its own sizes.** The Icons view's icon size, the Compact List's column width and the Previews view's count belong to the pane, at each density, as its view and order do, and so do the List's column widths, a folder's and search results' apart: the slider and Make Bigger, Make Smaller and Default Size change the active pane alone, as a divider dragged, stepped or double-clicked in the List changes the pane it is in, and the other pane, the other tabs and the other windows keep theirs. The size set last, in any pane, is the app's (`iconSizes`, `compactListWidths`, `previewCounts`, `detailColumnWidths`, `searchColumnWidths` in the preferences): a pane with none of its own takes it as its first folder is read, and has its own from then on. A pane opened from another takes that pane's sizes with its view: the second pane, New Tab, New Window, Duplicate Tab, Reopen Closed Tab. They are saved with the pane (`viewSizes`, in a saved tab and in its second pane), so the tabs brought back at launch and a workspace show each pane at its sizes; a file from before has none, and its panes start from the app's. One size for the whole app, kept for each density, is what there was: a workspace could not bring its sizes back, and a size set for one folder's photos changed every other pane. The List's column widths were the window's, shared by every tab and both panes of it, from before there were panes, and were left there when the other sizes moved: a Name column widened for one folder's long names widened it in the other pane and every tab, as a size did. A pane saved with sizes but no widths takes its window's as they were; a window's session no longer keeps any (decided 2026-11-15).
- **A size step and the selection.** A new icon size or column width keeps the item at the top left of the view (the Icons view) or the column at its left (the Compact List) in place. The selection's lead stays in view if it was in view just before the step; one out of view is left there, and later layouts don't bring it back until it is shown again.
- **A folder opened afresh in the List starts at its top and its left**, an empty one too; Back and Forward put back where it was scrolled. A rename with Name scrolled out of view scrolls back to the left first.
- **Previews is the fourth view.** View ▸ as Previews (⌘4) and the fourth segment of the toolbar's view switch show a pane's files as large Quick Look previews in a grid that scrolls down; each pane, and search results, choose it as they choose any view. View ▸ Make Bigger, Make Smaller and Default Size ("Make Previews Bigger"…) and the status bar's slider, with four stops, go between 9, 4, 2 and 1 previews at a time, in 3 by 3, 2 by 2, 2 by 1 and 1 by 1; 4 is the default, and the count is the pane's own (see "Each pane has its own sizes"). View ▸ Show Previews has 9, 4, 2 and 1 at a Time, each switching the active pane to Previews at that count from any view, search results too, or changing the count in Previews; it is saved as the slider's is, and checked while the pane shows Previews at it. They have no keys until one is chosen in Settings, as Sort By's items, so a file selected in the List is one key from filling the pane. The selected file is brought on screen every time, in Previews already and scrolled away from it too, as the commands are for showing that file at a size; the slider and Make Bigger keep the pane's place. Pressed again, an item changes nothing else, rather than going back to the view before, so its key and its checkmark mean one thing whatever the view. A submenu and not four items of the View menu's own, which is long already; menu items and not keys in Settings alone, so the menu shows the key chosen and the count on screen. The toolbar's view switch has the same four in its Previews segment, the system's menu of a segment: a click switches to Previews as before, a press held shows the menu, as Back and Forward's do. The system's arrow was tried and dropped: it made the segment wider than the other three, and a click on it didn't open the menu by itself. The size shown over the status text is as wide as it reads ("9 at a time"), not only as "600 pt". The places share the pane's width and height exactly, with no padding above or below, so a row at the top shows the shape's previews whole and nothing of the next; the preview size follows the pane, so a pane half as wide shows as many, smaller. A trackpad swipe that ends between rows settles on the next row the way it went once it went a sixth of a row, otherwise back; the arrows move a row at a time. Each picture fills its place in its own proportions, never stretched, with a 6 pt margin and its name right under it, and the selection hugs the picture. Everything else is the Icons view's: selection, keys, drags, rename, every command. Previews are asked of Quick Look at their place's size in the screen's pixels, in steps of 256, at most the screen's longer side; while the pane's size changes (a window growing to full size, an edge dragged) they keep the pictures they have and are asked for at the new size once it has rested a quarter of a second, not at every step on the way. A preview being made goes on when the folder is read again or the pane resized, and answers the same file asked for again, rather than being stopped and begun again each time. A file selected in another view is on screen when the pane switches to Previews. Quick Look gives no picture of a photo, a video or a drawing above about 3.5 million pixels: an image larger than that is read by the app itself, and of any other file the largest picture Quick Look gives is shown. The page past the one on screen, the way the pane last went, and then the page before it are asked for ahead of need, behind every preview on screen and leaving one load free for one, so a page scrolled to shows its previews at once; they go on loading when the folder is read again. No more than two pictures too large for Quick Look are decoded at once, and one scrolled past while it waits is not decoded at all, so flicking through a folder of very large pictures never has more than two going. A preview not known yet leaves its place empty for a fifth of a second, and is drawn there if it comes by then; otherwise the file's icon shows until it comes, so a preview that comes quickly isn't drawn over the icon a moment after it. They are kept apart from the Icons view's (the List and the Compact List show icons only), at most 1 GB or a sixteenth of the Mac's memory when that is less, counted by their pictures' bytes, the least recently used going first; every picture is kept at 8 bits a color, 4 bytes a pixel, whatever the file's depth, so an image of a high dynamic range shows its standard range; all go when macOS says memory is short. Quick Look keeps its own cache on disk, which the app neither sizes nor clears. A separate triage mode with single-letter keys and an Apply step, kind chips in the filter, a Trash Others on Page command, retake groups, copying an image or its text, renaming from the text in a picture, and live previews (a PDF's pages, a video playing) were each considered and dropped. A magnifier while ⌥ was held over a preview was built and removed: it showed little the preview at full size doesn't.
- **Quick Look is ⌘Y and Space**, as in Finder. ⌘Y comes first, so the File menu and Help show it (the menu can't show Space) and the toolbar's tooltip names it.
- **With hidden files shown, Quick Look's button and the hidden-files button both show an eye.** Kept as is.
- **The folders drawn by the system are the real special folders, by path:** the startup disk's `/Applications`, `/Developer`, `/Library`, `/System` and `/Users`, and the home folder's Applications, Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Library, Movies, Music, Pictures and Public. A folder of one of these names elsewhere is an ordinary folder, tag color and all.
- **Listings and naming use one list of 37 package extensions.** `.rtfd`, `.xcworkspace`, `.photoslibrary`, `.pkg`, `.sparsebundle`, `.key` and the rest show as one item, not a folder, and carry the system's kind ("Keynote Presentation"). Before, listings knew 10 and called the others folders.
- **The startup disk is shown by its real name**, as other disks already were, so a renamed startup disk is no longer called "Macintosh HD". The name is read at launch and on every rename, off the main thread, and open windows follow; the Go menu's item follows it too. Help and the startup disk's row in Settings' Shortcuts keep "Macintosh HD": they name a command, as Go ▸ Home does, and the Shortcuts label also feeds Help's golden.
