# Decisions: Views and what they show

The List, Icons, Compact List and Previews views, their sizes, Quick Look, and how items are named and drawn. Part of the owner's decisions: [the index](../decisions.md) lists the other parts, and [principles.md](../principles.md) says how a decision is weighed and when to ask about one.

- **A folder's items, and search results, can be put in groups** (2026-11-20). View ▸ Group By, in every view: a folder by name (the first letter), Date Modified, Date Created, Date Last Opened, Date Added (Today, Yesterday, Previous 7 Days, Previous 30 Days, this year's earlier months, then each year, counted from midnight and grouped again at the next), size (bands of a thousand, Zero bytes to Over 10 GB) or kind (broad kinds from the item's type), or tags (the first tag). The groups keep their own order whatever the sort, the newest dates and the smallest sizes first and the rest A to Z, and the sort orders the items inside each group, as in Finder; the owner found the sort's direction ordering the groups too, first built that way, made no sense. Titles are the name alone in the sidebar's heading style: Finder's layout with the app's own fonts, the List as Finder's (the first group's name in the Name column's title, an empty row and a title before each other group). The Compact List's groups follow one another down a column, each under its title over a hairline as wide as the column, and go on in the next column only when there is no room left: a title with no room under it for an item starts the next column, and a column that goes on with a group starts with its title again, fainter, so every column names what it holds; ← and → go to the item at the same height in the column beside. Until 2026-11-20 each group started a column of its own under a title held at the left edge as its columns scrolled; the owner found the columns disjointed. View ▸ Group By ▸ Reverse Group Order turns the groups around, the sort inside them unchanged, the groups without a value still last; a folder's view keeps it with the grouping. Groups don't collapse: chevrons, Collapse All and Expand All were built and taken out, the owner seeing no need. The pane carries its grouping as its order, a saved tab and a workspace keep it, and a folder's view can keep it as a sixth setting. Search results group by every field, Folder after Name, and sort by a date or Size as well as by name, folder and kind: a date, the size or the tags are read for every result in the background, a batch at a time, only while the results are ordered or grouped by them, and only that field; until a result's value comes it is with those without one ("Nothing is read before it is needed"). A first step had results group by name, folder and kind only, as their order then did. The plan, with what was changed on the way, is [records/group-by.md](../records/group-by.md).
- **The List has a Date Added column, and sorts by it** (2026-11-19). When each item was put in its folder, as Finder's Date Added: what Downloads is most often sorted by. Hidden until shown from the column header's right-click menu or View ▸ Columns, as Date Created is; View ▸ Sort By, the toolbar's Sort menu, the right-click Sort By and the column's header sort by it, newest first. The folder read gives it with the other dates (`ATTR_CMN_ADDEDTIME`), so sorting by it costs nothing more. Disks that don't keep it (FAT, exFAT, most shares) leave it empty, and such items sort last. Search results show it but don't sort by it, as with Date Created. The Info panel doesn't show it, as Finder's Get Info doesn't.
- **The List has a Date Last Opened column, and sorts by it** (2026-11-19). What macOS records when a file is opened (the `com.apple.lastuseddate#PS` attribute, which Finder's Date Last Opened shows). Hidden until shown from the column header's right-click menu or View ▸ Columns. It isn't in the folder read: it is read for the rows on screen, and for every item only while the folder is sorted by it, as sizes are. Items never opened show nothing and sort last. The home folder's Desktop, Documents and Downloads folders show none of their own, as reading them asks for permission (see Finder tags). Sort By lists the fields in the columns' order (see "Fields and columns are listed in one order").
- **List view's columns keep the widths they were given.** A pane narrower than the columns (a narrow window, the Info panel open) scrolls sideways; no column shrinks or hides to fit, and Name is never squeezed. A column dragged past the pane's edge scrolls the list so its divider stays in view. The list never gets narrower than what it shows to the right: a column narrowed with the list scrolled to its right end, as after a wide Name, leaves empty room past the last column, during the drag and after it, and the list gives that room up as it is scrolled back, so nothing moves under the pointer nor when it lets go. Before, the narrower list scrolled back under the pointer, which the drag took for the pointer moving on, and the column closed up to its smallest at once; closing the list up when the drag ended was tried first and dropped, as the columns then moved at the release (2026-11-15). The List's own scrolls (a divider past the pane's edge, a place put back, a page) go through the scroll view rather than its clip view alone: macOS keeps its own record of where a trackpad left a list, and put the list back there, in one jump, the next time its width changed. Widths change only when someone drags, steps or double-clicks a divider, so what is set is what shows.
- **The Icons view's icon size and the Compact List's column width are set with one slider in the status bar**, and with View ▸ Make Bigger, Make Smaller and Default Size (⌘+, ⌘- and ⌘0, changeable in Settings; ⌘= is ⌘+'s other key, as most keyboards type + with Shift): icons from 32 to 128 pt in steps of 8, columns from 160 to 600 pt, the commands going to the next whole 40. The List has none. Each pane has its own sizes, at each density, with the Previews view's count (see "Each pane has its own sizes"). The items are named for the view ("Make Icons Bigger", "Make Columns Wider") and are dimmed in the List and at the ends of the range. While the slider moves, or after one of the commands, the size shows in points in place of the status text, and fades once it stops. Dragging the gaps between columns was tried and dropped: nothing on screen shows the gaps can be dragged. Icons larger than 64 pt ask Quick Look for 256-pixel previews instead of 128. An item's column is its icon plus 40 pt, 104 at the default 64, and grows and narrows with the icon, but never below 80 pt: smaller icons fit more items across, and a name still has a 78 pt label for its two lines. Holding the column at 104 below 64 pt, so smaller icons only made the rows shorter, was what there was; no floor at all would leave a name 70 pt at 32 pt icons.
- **Each pane has its own sizes.** The Icons view's icon size, the Compact List's column width and the Previews view's count belong to the pane, at each density, as its view and order do, and so do the List's column widths, a folder's and search results' apart: the slider and Make Bigger, Make Smaller and Default Size change the active pane alone, as a divider dragged, stepped or double-clicked in the List changes the pane it is in, and the other pane, the other tabs and the other windows keep theirs. The size set last, in any pane, is the app's (`iconSizes`, `compactListWidths`, `previewCounts`, `detailColumnWidths`, `searchColumnWidths` in the preferences): a pane with none of its own takes it as its first folder is read, and has its own from then on. A pane opened from another takes that pane's sizes with its view: the second pane, New Tab, New Window, Duplicate Tab, Reopen Closed Tab. They are saved with the pane (`viewSizes`, in a saved tab and in its second pane), so the tabs brought back at launch and a workspace show each pane at its sizes; a file from before has none, and its panes start from the app's. One size for the whole app, kept for each density, is what there was: a workspace could not bring its sizes back, and a size set for one folder's photos changed every other pane. The List's column widths were the window's, shared by every tab and both panes of it, from before there were panes, and were left there when the other sizes moved: a Name column widened for one folder's long names widened it in the other pane and every tab, as a size did. A pane saved with sizes but no widths takes its window's as they were; a window's session no longer keeps any (decided 2026-11-15).
- **A folder can keep its own view** (2026-11-19). View ▸ Folder View ▸ Remember View for This Folder keeps five settings of the folder on screen: its view, its order (the field and the direction together), Folders First, the List's columns (which, in what order, and their widths) and its size (the icon size, the Compact List's column width and the Previews count, at the density on screen). A pane still carries its own settings from folder to folder, as before; a folder's view is laid over them while the pane shows that folder, and leaving the folder puts back what the pane carried. So a photo folder kept in Previews doesn't bring Previews into Downloads, and a folder with no view of its own behaves as it always did. A setting a folder's view doesn't keep follows the pane. Hidden files are not part of a folder's view: they stay the tab's (see "A tab can show two panes side by side"). Search results are not a folder and keep their own view. Considered and dropped: a fixed default view every other folder opens in, as Finder's Use as Defaults (it would end the pane carrying its settings, so ⌘1 in Downloads would last until you left); remembering every folder visited, as Finder does (a quick look in Icons would change a folder for good); hidden files per folder.
- **Folder View Options chooses what a folder's view keeps, and saves what is changed** (2026-11-19). View ▸ Folder View ▸ Folder View Options… (⌘J, Finder's key for its View Options), the status bar's symbol and the menus open one popover, laid out as Settings lays out a page: a header with the folder's icon, Folder View Options and the folder's name; Keep for This Folder, a row for each setting, a checkbox titled with its name on the left (a click on the name checks it, as on any Mac; a box with a separate label didn't) and its control in a column that starts at the same place in every row, each control at its own width, dimmed while its box is unchecked; then an Apply to Subfolders row, its switch at the right end and what it does under its name; then Reset, All Folder Views…, Revert and Save. The popover is one color, the window's content color (white in the light appearance), with hairlines between the rows. There are always four setting rows, so the popover keeps its height as the view changes: View, a pop-up of the four views with the toolbar's symbols; Sort By, a pop-up of every field, a column shown or not, beside one of Ascending and Descending, each as wide for every choice; Folders First, a pop-up of Before Files and Mixed with Files, as a switch for it said little either way; and the List's Column Visibility and Order in the List (no control: the List's header sets them), the icon size in Icons, the column width in the Compact List, and how many at a time (9 (3 × 3), 4 (2 × 2), 2 (2 × 1), 1) in Previews. What is changed in it, a value, a Keep box or Apply to Subfolders, is a change on screen as any other (⌘2, a column, the slider): it shows at once, Save keeps it, Revert drops it, and so does leaving the folder. A changed row has an orange dot by its name, whose tooltip says what is saved (of the size, the sizes that differ: "Saved: Previews 9 at a time"). In a folder with no view, checking a box or changing a value makes one on screen; Save saves it, and it goes when nothing of it is changed any more. With every box unchecked and Apply to Subfolders off, Save removes the folder's view. Reset is that change, on screen until Save as any other: it unchecks every box and turns Apply to Subfolders off, and the folder shows the pane's settings; Revert brings the customization back. It removed the customization at once at first; the owner wanted it to wait for Save as everything else in the popover does (2026-11-19). View ▸ Folder View ▸ Reset Folder View still removes it at once, as Remember saves at once: those commands are the saving. Revert and Save are dimmed with nothing changed, Reset in a folder without a view of its own or with nothing left to reset, so the buttons don't come and go. The popover is the same in every folder. In a folder that uses a view from above, a line under the header says whose the settings are and that a change here customizes this folder on its own, with a Go to button to the folder above, where that folder is changed; Save gives this folder a view of its own, the one it used with the changes, and Apply to Subfolders starts off, as covering the folders inside is the folder above's. Saving to the folder above from below it, and a Customize This Folder button, were there first: Customize This Folder did nothing with nothing changed, and once used it had saved, so Save was dimmed; the owner found two ways to save from a subfolder unclear, as Go to already leads to the folder above (2026-11-19). The folder above's popover no longer lists the folders inside it customized on their own: Folder Views shows them, under it. No control has the keyboard as it opens. There was a second popover, Unsaved Changes, listing what was changed beside what was saved, with Update and Revert, opened from the symbol while something was changed; and the options saved each change at once. The owner asked why there were two for the same settings, and two rules for saving: one dialog, where everything is saved with Save, replaced them (2026-11-19). An earlier version said whose view it was in a subtitle (“this folder's view”), named the checkbox “Also for the folders inside it”, listed the columns shown, cut the view's names and the direction to fit, had a slider for the size, arrows for the direction and a pop-up that changed width with its choice, put the controls in a plain grid, grey cards on a white ground, Folders First as a switch, the view as four symbols with no Previews counts, the subfolders' explanation as a line apart from their switch, the controls each pushed to the right edge and then all stretched to one width, and five rows in some views and three in others, and once listed only the orders of columns shown; the owner found the words unclear and the look cheap (2026-11-19). It was called Folder Customization, its button All Customizations… and the window Folder Customizations, beside menus that said Folder View; the owner had one name used everywhere: the popover is Folder View Options, as its menu item is, the button All Folder Views…, the window and its menu item Folder Views and Manage Folder Views…, and the words say a folder has a view of its own, not that it is customized (2026-11-20).
- **A folder's view is saved only when asked** (2026-11-19). In a folder with a view, switching the view, sorting, Folders First, a column or a size changes only what is on screen; Update Folder View (Save in Folder View Options) saves it, Revert to Saved View drops it, and leaving the folder drops it too, without a question, whether by another folder, Back, closing the tab or quitting. Coming back shows what is saved. Changing a setting back by hand is no change. A setting the folder's view doesn't keep is not a change to the folder: the pane carries it on, as anywhere. Saving is done by Remember, Save (Update Folder View) and View ▸ Folder View ▸ Reset Folder View, nothing else: a Keep box, Apply to Subfolders, Folder View Options's Reset and Keep Columns for … in the column header's menu are changes on screen, saved with Save. The owner chose this over saving each change as it was made, which was simpler to describe but made every quick look a lasting change and left the subfolder question to be guessed at every change. A question on leaving was not considered: it would add a step to the commonest thing done.
- **A pane or tab opened from another shows a customized folder's saved view** (2026-11-20). Duplicate Pane, Duplicate Tab and Reopen Closed Tab show the folder's view as it is saved, not changes that were on screen and unsaved in the pane they come from: a change is kept only with Save, and is dropped whenever the folder is left.
- **A setting a folder starts to keep is given back to the pane** (2026-11-19). Before a folder is customized, what is changed in it (switching to List, a sort, a column) is the pane's, and would be carried to every folder after it. So the pane notes what it carried as it came into the folder, and once the folder keeps a setting (Remember, or Save after a Keep box or Keep Columns for …), the pane goes back to its noted value of that setting; for the columns and sizes, every folder's (the app's) go back too, as a change there went to them. A setting the folder doesn't keep stays as the pane has it. Before, the first Remember left the pane with the folder's view, and every folder after it showed it: the owner found that annoying. Of every folder's columns and sizes, the pane puts back only what it changed itself in that folder, to what it was before its change, and only where it is still as the pane left it; what was changed elsewhere meanwhile (a column shown from another tab or window) stays. Putting back all of them as they were when the pane came in was what there was, and Remember or Save in one tab hid a column shown from another since (the owner's choice, 2026-11-20).
- **A folder's view can cover the folders inside it, and the nearest view wins whole** (2026-11-19). With Apply to Subfolders, every folder below that has no view of its own uses it. A folder uses one view only: its own, or else the nearest folder above whose view covers the folders inside, never settings from several. A view that keeps nothing and covers the folders inside is how a branch stops using a view from above (Pictures/Work, inside Pictures, shows the pane's settings). Apply to Subfolders with nothing kept is a change only where it does that (2026-11-20): where the nearest covering view above keeps something, and Folder View Options then says the folders inside stop using that folder's view. Anywhere else the folders inside would look no different, so the switch is dimmed until a setting is checked, and goes off with the last setting unchecked; before, it lit Save and made a customization that changed nothing. Save (Update Folder View) in a folder that uses a view from above gives that folder its own view, a copy of the one it used with the change; the folder above is changed in it, reached by Go to. Update first asked which, this folder alone or the folder above, and the folder above's options listed the folders inside with their own view; the owner had both dropped as more than the case needs (2026-11-19). Folder Views lists the folders inside with their own view, under the folder above, and Remove there has them use it again. Settings looked up one at a time across levels was drawn first and dropped: a folder could take its view from one folder and its order from another, and nothing on screen could say why it looked as it did.
- **A folder's view shows as a symbol at the right end of the status bar** (2026-11-19). About 18 pt, left of the size slider, in every folder: it stays in its place whether or not the view has a slider, so a popover opened from it doesn't move as the view changes (beside the count, it moved with the slider's coming and going). It is in the accent color when the folder has its own view, the label's color when its view comes from a folder above, grey when it has none, with an orange dot while there are unsaved changes; the symbol is 13.5 pt in its 18 pt place. It was 12 pt, grey from above and faint with none: faint, the way to the options in most folders was easy to miss, so none became grey, and from above a step stronger so the two still differ (2026-11-20). Search results have none, and neither has a pane that let go of its folder; Folder View Options, open when the symbol goes, closes with it, where its Keep boxes went on changing the view of a folder not on screen (2026-11-20). Its tooltip says whose view it is and whether something is unsaved. A click opens Folder View Options, as the menus and ⌘J do, and they open it from the symbol too. A pill with words (“Folder's own view · Changed”) was drawn first and dropped: it took 130 to 190 pt from the path beside it. The symbol was first shown only where a folder had a view; the owner had it always there, as a popover opened from the menu then rose from the item count, and the symbol is a way to the options in any folder (2026-11-19).
- **The Folder View commands are a submenu of View, of the list's right-click menu and of the toolbar's View Options menu** (2026-11-19). View ▸ Folder View has Remember View for This Folder, Update Folder View, Revert to Saved View, Folder View Options… (⌘J), Reset Folder View and Manage Folder Views…; the list background's right-click menu and the toolbar's View Options menu (after Hidden Files) have the same submenu, and an item's right-click menu doesn't (“this folder” would be ambiguous there). A section with its own heading in the View Options menu was tried first and dropped: no other part of that menu has a heading, and it differed from the right-click menu for no reason. A Folder View toolbar button is in Customize Toolbar and on no toolbar until added, as Duplicate Pane's is. Update and Revert have no keys until one is chosen in Settings, as Sort By's items. The names are whole (“Update Folder View”, not “Update”) as the command palette, Help and Settings → Shortcuts show them alone. Manage Folder Views… (All Folder Views… in Folder View Options) opens a window of its own, Folder Views, that lists every customized folder as a tree: a folder customized inside another is under it, as the view it uses comes from there, the home folder's at the top first. A row is one line: the folder's icon and its path, and on the right tags of what it keeps, Subfolders when it applies to them, and Not Found or Disk Not Connected; on a selected row the path and tags are white. An empty list says how to customize a folder. Remove forgets the selected folders' customizations (⌫ too), and the folders customized inside them keep theirs and move up the tree; Remove with Subfolders forgets those of every folder inside each selected folder too, at any depth, and nothing above, beside or outside a selected one, whatever folders are selected; neither asks, as each says what it does. Remove All… forgets every one after asking. Open, Return or a double-click opens the folder in the window it was asked from, and Esc closes the window. A plain table of paths with columns of words was built first; the owner found it cheap, flat and missing Esc; then rows of two lines (name, then path) under a line of explanation, with one Remove; the owner found the rows needlessly tall, the explanation needless, a selected row's tags unreadable, "Folder Views" the wrong name, and wanted a choice about the folders inside (2026-11-19). It was first a list in Settings → Browsing; the owner wanted it apart from Settings (2026-11-19).
- **The List's columns are chosen on its header** (2026-11-19). A right-click on a column title lists the columns to check, in the one order of fields and columns, Name always on; Keep Columns for “…” first, which makes them the folder's own, in a folder using a view from above that keeps columns too: choosing it there gives the folder a view of its own that keeps the columns on screen, unsaved until Save as any change, and the item shows checked (before, it did nothing there; the owner's choice, 2026-11-20); Move “Size” Left and Move “Size” Right for the title clicked; Size All Columns to Fit; and Reset Columns. A title dragged sideways moves its column, once the pointer has gone 4 pt, so a click still sorts and a drag at a divider still resizes; Name stays first. View ▸ Columns has the same checkable items. Where a change goes is said under the first item: to every folder without columns of its own, saved as before, or to the folder's view, unsaved until Update. Search results have the same menu without the Keep item, Move Left and Move Right with it, and a title dragged there moves the results' own column (their order is `searchColumnOrder`), Folder as any other, Name staying first; a drag there had moved the folder List's column, and nothing moved the results' since the lists left Settings (2026-11-20). Settings → Browsing no longer has the two column checklists (they had been there since the first version). An Edit Columns popover and a button at the header's end were drawn and dropped: the header's own menu is where a Mac list keeps its columns, and the header scrolls sideways, so a button there would scroll away.
- **Fields and columns are listed in one order** (2026-11-19): Name, Date Modified, Date Created, Date Last Opened, Date Added, Size, Kind, Permissions (search results put Folder after Name). View ▸ Sort By, the toolbar's Sort menu, the right-click Sort By, Folder View Options's order, the column header's menu, View ▸ Columns, Settings → Shortcuts, the command palette and Help all list them so, and a column shown takes its place in it among the columns shown: right after the nearest one shown that comes before it, or first after Name. A column moved by hand stays where it was moved; one moved, hidden and shown again comes back in its standard place, as remembering a hidden column's place would need a record of which columns were moved by hand. Before, a hidden column kept a place in the saved order, and an order saved before the standard order changed put Date Created after Kind. It is Finder's order in its column header's menu. Sort By had Finder's own order for that menu (Name, Kind, Date Last Opened, Date Added, Date Modified, Size), which differs from Finder's header menu; the owner wanted the same order everywhere. Date Created sorts too, the newest first, from the times the folder read gives with each item's modification time: it was first in the column lists only, and the owner found it missing from Sort By (2026-11-19).
- **Folders' views are kept in `folder-views.json`, and a folder's view is found by its full path only** (2026-11-19; found by the path alone since 2026-11-20). Beside `app-state.json` in Application Support, as `visited-folders.json` is, and nothing is written into the folders: no `.DS_Store`, so views work on disks and shares that can't be written. A view is its folder's full path and what it keeps, nothing else, and finding the view of a folder being opened is a look in memory: no call to the disk, however many views are saved. The folder is looked up by the path it was asked for by before it is read, as the read needs the order the view keeps; its listing then gives its real path, and where that is another (a link, `/tmp`) the view saved for the real path is the one shown, the folder read once more only where that view's order differs. So a folder reached through a link still has one view. A rename or move made in File Trail, and its Undo, take the folder's view and every view below it along, to another disk too. Where a view goes along, the place the folder went is resolved to its real path once (a folder dropped on a link to a folder would otherwise have its view saved under the link's path, where no folder finds it); a move that carries no view asks the disk nothing, and opening a folder never does (2026-11-20). The views of one operation's folders are all taken from where they were before any is put where it went, so a batch rename that hands names along or swaps them leaves each folder its own view (2026-11-20). Accepted with this: a folder renamed or moved outside File Trail loses its view, and its entry stays in Folder Views as Not Found until removed; and a folder at the same path on another disk of the same name uses the same view. Views are never removed on their own: Folder Views lists those not found, checking each listed folder on the disk as the window opens (kept as it is, 2026-11-20). This replaced a view found by its disk (the startup disk, another disk's volume UUID, or a share's server and name) and its canonical path on it, with the folder's file ID kept too, so that a folder opened with no view took over one whose folder was gone and whose ID was its, which found it again after a rename or move in Finder: opening a folder then resolved its path, read its file ID and looked up its disk on the main thread whenever any view was saved, and the owner decided a folder customization should not need any additional lookups. A file written before has a `volume` and a `fileID` with each view; it loads, and they are dropped at the next write. A view's columns, widths and sizes are read one by one, a missing one being as it first is, so a column added to the app costs no folder the columns it keeps; a file that can't be read at all is reported as the app's other files are. Such a file is renamed to `folder-views.json.unreadable` beside it (in place of an older one of that name) just before the first view saved since would be written over it, so nothing in it is lost and it can be mended by hand; a file that read, or a missing one, is never renamed, and where the rename fails the write goes ahead and the failure is reported (the owner's choice, 2026-11-20). Before, the next Remember, Save or followed move wrote a file holding only the new view over it. Kept in `app-state.json`, it would be written with every tab and window change; SQLite was more than a few hundred entries need; an extended attribute on each folder writes into the folders and fails on many disks and shares.
- **A size step and the selection.** A new icon size or column width keeps the item at the top left of the view (the Icons view) or the column at its left (the Compact List) in place. The selection's lead stays in view if it was in view just before the step; one out of view is left there, and later layouts don't bring it back until it is shown again.
- **A folder opened afresh in the List starts at its top and its left**, an empty one too; Back and Forward put back where it was scrolled. A rename with Name scrolled out of view scrolls back to the left first.
- **Previews is the fourth view.** View ▸ as Previews (⌘4) and the fourth segment of the toolbar's view switch show a pane's files as large Quick Look previews in a grid that scrolls down; each pane, and search results, choose it as they choose any view. View ▸ Make Bigger, Make Smaller and Default Size ("Make Previews Bigger"…) and the status bar's slider, with four stops, go between 9, 4, 2 and 1 previews at a time, in 3 by 3, 2 by 2, 2 by 1 and 1 by 1; 4 is the default, and the count is the pane's own (see "Each pane has its own sizes"). View ▸ Show Previews has 9, 4, 2 and 1 at a Time, each switching the active pane to Previews at that count from any view, search results too, or changing the count in Previews; it is saved as the slider's is, and checked while the pane shows Previews at it. They have no keys until one is chosen in Settings, as Sort By's items, so a file selected in the List is one key from filling the pane. The selected file is brought on screen every time, in Previews already and scrolled away from it too, as the commands are for showing that file at a size; the slider and Make Bigger keep the pane's place. Arriving at 1 at a time that way, the file the pane then shows is selected, as a scroll that came to rest on it would select it, unless several files are selected: before, a selected file scrolled away from at 4 at a time stayed the one Move to Trash acted on while another filled the pane (2026-11-20). Pressed again, an item changes nothing else, rather than going back to the view before, so its key and its checkmark mean one thing whatever the view. A submenu and not four items of the View menu's own, which is long already; menu items and not keys in Settings alone, so the menu shows the key chosen and the count on screen. The toolbar's view switch has the same four in its Previews segment, the system's menu of a segment: a click switches to Previews as before, a press held shows the menu, as Back and Forward's do. At 1 at a time the file a scroll rests on is selected, as ↓ would select it, so Space, the Info panel and Move to Trash act on the file on screen and not on one scrolled away from; a selection of several files is kept, and so is one a drag made as it scrolled the pane, or one being renamed. When the selected file is removed (Move to Trash, a move, a delete in Finder), the file that takes its place on screen is selected, the one before it when it was the last: Move to Trash pressed again acts on the file shown, where it did nothing until a scroll or ↓; a held ⌘⌫ still trashes one file, as a held key repeats no command that starts a write (decided 2026-11-20). Quick Look gives no picture of a photo, a video or a drawing above about 3.5 million pixels: an image larger than that is read by the app itself, and of any other file the largest picture Quick Look gives is shown. The page past the one on screen, the way the pane last went, and then the page before it are asked for ahead of need, behind every preview on screen and leaving one load free for one, so a page scrolled to shows its previews at once; they go on loading when the folder is read again. No more than two pictures too large for Quick Look are decoded at once, and one scrolled past while it waits is not decoded at all, so flicking through a folder of very large pictures never has more than two going. A preview not known yet leaves its place empty for a fifth of a second, and is drawn there if it comes by then; otherwise the file's icon shows until it comes, so a preview that comes quickly isn't drawn over the icon a moment after it. They are kept apart from the Icons view's (the List and the Compact List show icons only), at most 1 GB or a sixteenth of the Mac's memory when that is less, counted by their pictures' bytes, the least recently used going first; every picture is kept at 8 bits a color, 4 bytes a pixel, whatever the file's depth, so an image of a high dynamic range shows its standard range; all go when macOS says memory is short.
